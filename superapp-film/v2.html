Why v1 doesn't flow

The thing that's hard to pin down is that v1 is built like a feature list, not a journey. Specifically:

v1

  • 47 shots averaging 3.8 s, each generated on its own. No shot was written to hand off to the next, so every cut is a jump.
  • Six chapter cards stop the film six times.
  • Every chapter repeats the same loop of problem, screen, fix. The rhythm becomes predictable by 1:00.
  • Each problem is fixed on the spot, so no tension carries into the next chapter.
  • The real story (the hoist bottleneck) only appears at 2:20.
  • The music stops and restarts, and the audience never knows where in the tower they are.
  • The future appears in only four short moments, so most of the film reads as today.

v2

  • The Cyan Thread. The camera follows one glowing signal between scenes, so every transition has a reason.
  • Hand-off frames. Every prompt ends on the frame the next one starts from. Clips are generated as a chain.
  • The Living Tower hub. Between chapters we pull out to the twin, an amber pin drops, and we dive into the next floor. It works as a map and a rhythm, and replaces the chapter cards.
  • Open loops. Each challenge is detected and explained, and the humans hold the line, but it stays amber. Tension builds up to 2:20.
  • The hoist is seeded in every chapter: waiting materials (Level 7), lift traffic (safety), the idle crew at the hoist (labor), and the queue (equipment).
  • One clock and one score: a single day, a single track with three planned drops.
  • The future is everywhere: glowing badges, cyan connectivity in every scene, floating light-mode UI, and Physical AI actions in the pay-off.

Chain method: how to generate for flow

  1. Keep the assets you already made. Cast Soul IDs and the keyframes marked Reuse v1 carry over. Generate the new keyframes first, especially K16 (Living Tower master), K17 (floating light-mode panel) and K06 (SENSE glow).
  2. Generate in film order, as a chain. Export the last frame of each finished clip and use it as the start frame of the next. The Hand-off line in each card says exactly what that frame must contain.
  3. Match cuts: where a hand-off says MATCH, generate both clips from the same keyframe composition (same framing, same subject position).
  4. Prompt order: base style (A or E) + layer style (B) + shot prompt + device style (C or D). The copy buttons assemble this for you. Put the negative prompt in its own field.
  5. Clips longer than 5 s are split into a/b parts with a hidden cut on movement. Each part has its own prompt.
  6. UI, text, logos and kanji are always added in post. Devices and panels are generated blank (light mode, with tracking dots), and real SuperApp recordings are tracked onto them.
  7. Lock the score before you edit. One continuous track, with cuts timed to its beats. A temp track is fine. This alone makes the film feel twice as fluid.

Global prompts

Style A · Ultra-real near-future live action Live

Photorealistic, ultra-detailed cinematic live action, ARRI Alexa 65 large-format look with anamorphic lenses, 24 fps, 4K HDR, subtle film grain, natural skin pores and fabric texture, realistic physics, motion blur and lens behaviour. Setting: an immaculate, orderly 30-storey high-rise construction site in central Tokyo in the near future: white perimeter panels, green scaffold mesh sheets, white electric tower cranes, a glass-walled site office, clean exposed concrete slabs. Japanese workers in authentic site workwear (white or yellow helmets with chin straps, full-body harnesses, tobi trousers, long-sleeve work jackets), each wearing a small sleek smart badge on the harness strap with a soft cyan status light. Premium product-film composition, calm, confident, awe-inspiring and real.

Style B · Cyan connectivity layer Cyan

A luminous cyan connectivity layer integrated into the real scene: thin glowing cyan lines with a soft bloom, small light particles travelling along them, gentle pulse rings expanding from smart badges, glowing contour outlines on machines and work zones. The light is depth-correct, passing behind and in front of objects and reflecting faintly on wet concrete, glass and helmets. Elegant, sparse, high-end technology product film. No sci-fi HUD clutter, no text, no numbers.

Style C · Device screen, light mode

The device screen glows bright white in light mode: a blank pale-grey interface area with four small dark tracking dots in its corners, no readable text, ready for screen replacement. Cool white screen light falls on the face and hands, with natural reflections on the glass.

Style D · Floating light-mode glass panels

Thin frosted-glass interface panels float in the air in front of the character in light mode: bright white surfaces with soft drop shadows and a fine glowing cyan edge, blank content areas with four small tracking dots for screen replacement. The panels unfold smoothly from the device like folding paper, with realistic depth of field, parallax and reflections. No readable text.

Style E · The Living Tower (AI illustration world) Twin

AI illustration world: a 30-storey tower rendered as a luminous digital twin of clear glass and fine cyan linework, floating in deep navy-black space. Floor slabs are thin glowing planes, people and machines are tiny particles of light, with soft volumetric glow, isometric precision and cinematic shallow depth of field. Amber glowing pins mark open issues and cyan pins mark resolved ones. Premium, calm, awe-inspiring. No text.

Negative prompt (every shot)

cartoon, anime, video game look, plastic CGI look, cluttered sci-fi HUD, neon cyberpunk, purple or pink glow, distorted faces, extra or missing fingers, changing character face or clothing, missing helmet, worker at height without harness, messy or dirty site, dark-mode user interface, generated user interface, readable generated text, gibberish kanji, logos, watermarks, burned-in subtitles, oversaturated colors, excessive lens flare, stock footage look, slideshow transitions

For S01–S31, also add "robots, autonomous machines" to the negative prompt. Robots appear only from S32 onward.
