SuperApp: From Physical Reality to Digital IntelligenceフィジカルAIで、現場の今を経営の判断へ

180 s · six 30 s blocks · 47 shots 16:9 · 4K · 24 fps (48 fps on slow-motion shots) Market: Japan & APAC · EN narration, JP subtitles Mix: ~70% live action · ~20% real screens · ~10% Physical AI illustration

This keeps the look of the Tokyo workshop film: serious, epic camerawork, match cuts, a moment where time stops, and real Japanese site rituals played straight with some quiet humor. This time every scene shows a real construction problem and the actual SuperApp screen that addresses it. We follow one site manager through one day on one tower in Tokyo, and his decisions are always his own.

How to generate this

  1. Build the cast first. Generate K01 (Arai) and K02 (Mori), then create a Soul ID from each. Apply it to every shot they appear in.
  2. Lock the look with five keyframes: K01, K02, K06 (illustration style), K12 (illustrated tower) and K14 (closing hero). If these hold up, the rest of the film will too.
  3. Generate image-to-video, one shot per prompt. Use each shot's keyframe or the last frame of the previous shot as the start frame. Shots longer than 5 s (S03, S39) can be generated as two clips and cut on movement.
  4. Paste in order: global style prompt + shot prompt, then the negative prompt in its own field. The copy buttons can add the style prompt for you (toggle below).
  5. Never generate UI, text, logos or kanji. Devices are generated with a blank dark screen and corner tracking dots (Style C). Real SuperApp recordings, the Trusstor badge, signage, chapter cards and subtitles are all added in post.
  6. Generate 2–3 takes per shot. Choose on performance first (Arai's stillness, sincere rituals) and physics second.
Tension in the brief, and how it's resolved: section 5 of the brief says "avoid unrealistic holograms and overly futuristic effects", but you want futuristic Physical AI visuals. These prompts handle it by never putting holograms into the scene. The futuristic material is a separate, clearly illustrated linework layer (Style B), used for five beats only: SENSE, CONNECT, the crew map, the two kinds of machine signal, and UNDERSTAND. The real world stays real, the product stays real, and the illustration shows the Physical AI idea. Confirm this with Omri before generating.

Global prompts

Style A · Live action Live

Photorealistic cinematic live action, shot on ARRI Alexa 35 with anamorphic lenses, 24 fps, 4K, subtle film grain, natural skin and fabric texture, realistic physics and motion blur. Setting: a large, orderly high-rise construction site in central Tokyo with white perimeter panels, green scaffold mesh sheets, tower cranes and exposed concrete slabs. Japanese workers in authentic site workwear: white or yellow helmets with chin straps, full-body harnesses, tobi trousers, long-sleeve work jackets. Premium documentary realism, calm and credible, nothing exaggerated.

Style B · Physical AI illustration layer Illus

Physical AI illustration layer over a darkened, slightly desaturated live-action plate. Fine luminous linework in white and teal-cyan, like a technical architectural ink drawing that draws itself in real time: thin contour outlines around people, machines and work zones, small concentric signal rings, dotted flow lines, isometric tick marks and a faint grid. Flat 2D graphic linework, precise and elegant, clearly an illustration. Not a 3D hologram, no glow bloom, no sci-fi HUD clutter, no text.

Style C · Screen plate (append to any shot with a device)

The device screen shows a flat, uniform dark-grey surface with four small white tracking dots at its corners: no interface, no text, ready for screen replacement. Natural reflections on the glass, realistic hands and finger contact.

Negative prompt (every shot)

cartoon, anime, video game look, plastic CGI look, 3D hologram, sci-fi HUD, neon glow, robots, autonomous machines operating on their own, futuristic vehicles, distorted faces, extra or missing fingers, changing character face or clothing, missing helmet, worker at height without harness, messy or dirty site, generated user interface, generated text, gibberish kanji, logos, watermarks, burned-in subtitles, oversaturated colors, excessive lens flare, stock footage look, slideshow transitions

Visual grammar

SENSE→CONNECT→UNDERSTAND→EMPOWER
  • The pipeline builds up across the film as a small GFX mark in the lower left: SENSE lights at S06, CONNECT at S09, UNDERSTAND at S39 and EMPOWER at S42. Each chapter card shows which stages that chapter uses.
  • Illustration rules: solid lines mean machine telemetry, dotted rings mean tracked assets and badges, dashed outlines mean zones, and amber means an event. Use one palette, white and teal. The layer only ever draws over a darkened live plate, or over charcoal when we are "inside" the data.
  • Four wow moments: S03 the still point (Arai stays still while the day time-lapses around him), S06 SENSE (time slows and the site draws itself), S39 into the picture (we push through the monitor into the illustrated tower), and S46 「ヨシ!」 at dusk.
  • Callbacks to the workshop film: the kigumi joinery click (S09), the morning calisthenics (S05, real people only this time) and pointing-and-calling (S46).
  • Chapter cards (EN/JP, in post) use the brief's six key messages, placed at 0:30, 0:55, 1:30, 1:50, 2:15 and 2:40.
  • Timing: the brief's storyboard is kept, with chapter boundaries moved by 5 s or less so the film splits into six even 30 s blocks.
